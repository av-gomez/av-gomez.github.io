Thesis: where do Peruvians go when they get sick?

What leads a person with a health need to choose public care, private care, a pharmacy or no care at all?

What I did

ENAHO Panel 2022-2024, people aged 18 to 65. Multinomial logit with correlated random effects (Mundlak approach), which separates what changes within a person across years from what differs between people.

I add provincial indicators of the health environment: appointment delay, travel time, waiting time and care experience. All in Stata and Python.

Undergraduate thesis in Economics, PUCP. Advisor: Pedro Francke. First place (1/87) in Thesis Seminar 1.

First figure: choice composition by percentile of real per capita income. Own elaboration with ENAHO Panel 2022-2024.

Thesis abstract

Translated from Spanish. Using the panel component of Peru's National Household Survey (ENAHO) 2022-2024, this research estimates the determinants of health provider choice in the face of a perceived need among Peruvians aged 18 to 65, distinguishing four alternatives: not seeking care, the pharmacy, the formal public system and the formal private system. The choice is modeled with a multinomial logit with correlated random effects under the Mundlak (1978) method, which corrects the bias from unobserved individual heterogeneity without giving up time-invariant structural variables. Provincial indicators of the health environment are added to the individual model (appointment delay, travel time, waiting room time and the relational experience with the system), built with the cross-sectional ENAHO of the same years, independent of the estimation sample. Three results structure the conclusions. First, need orders the choice: acute shocks strongly reduce not seeking care, and chronic relapse is the only shock that shifts demand from the pharmacy counter to the formal system, while the choice of pharmacy is inelastic to household income. Second, the segmentation of the system is structural: use of the private sector responds to a person's usual income level and not to annual fluctuations, and the effect of insurance, the dominant enabling factor (affiliation with the Comprehensive Health Insurance, SIS, is associated with 17.2 percentage points more use of the public system and 13.8 points less non-seeking), is predominantly a difference between individuals rather than a change within the individual. Third, the territorial environment shifts demand across channels: provincial appointment delay diverts demand to the pharmacy, travel time erodes use of the public system, waiting room time pushes toward not seeking care, and distrust and mistreatment in the environment specifically penalize the public system without deactivating demand. The thesis concludes that the nominal expansion of insurance solves initial contact with the system, but does not close the gaps in perceived quality or the time costs that segment the use of health services in Peru.
